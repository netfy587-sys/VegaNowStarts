Chapter 1Why a balcony garden

You do not need a yard to grow things. A window ledge, a sunny corner or a few pots on a balcony are enough to start. Plants freshen the air, add colour, and bring a small daily ritual of watering, watching and waiting.

Not everything powerful has to look complicated. A few pots, some soil and a bit of sunlight can become the calmest corner of your home. Small changes create big impacts: one tiny plant changes how a space feels.

Anytime, Anywhere, Anything: whether you live in a flat, a hostel or a family house, there is a plant that suits your space.

Full sun (6+ hours)

Tulsi (holy basil), chilli, marigold and tomato love bright light. Water regularly, especially in hot months.

Partial sun (3–5 hours)

Mint, coriander, spinach and money plant do well here. They enjoy morning light and cooler afternoons.

Low light (under 3 hours)

Snake plant, ZZ plant, pothos and peace lily are patient and easy. Avoid overwatering.

Chapter 5Your first-weekend plan

Try it out today and you'll never be disappointed. Start with these five steps.

  1. Watch the light. Note how many hours of sun your balcony gets during the day.
  2. Pick three plants. Start small. Three healthy plants beat ten struggling ones.
  3. Choose pots with drainage. Holes at the bottom stop roots from rotting.
  4. Fill and plant. Use good potting mix, plant gently and water slowly until it drains out.
  5. Set a routine. Check soil each morning with a finger. Water only when the top layer feels dry.

Chapter 8Small glossary

Potting mix
A light soil blend made for containers, better than heavy garden soil.
Drainage
The ability of extra water to flow out through holes at the pot's base.
Repotting
Moving a plant to a bigger pot when roots run out of space.
Compost
Natural, decomposed kitchen waste that feeds the soil.

Chapter 9Quick answers

I forget to water. Can I still garden?

Yes. Choose hardy plants like snake plant or pothos, and set a phone reminder.

Do I need fertiliser?

Beginners can start with good potting mix and add mild compost every month or two.

Why are my leaves turning yellow?

Overwatering is the most common cause. Check drainage and let the soil dry slightly.

Can I garden with pets?

Yes, but check that every plant is pet-safe, since some common houseplants can be harmful.
